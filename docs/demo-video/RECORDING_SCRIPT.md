# Bridge Builder — three-minute demo script

A recording script for team sAI. Target: 3:00, approximately 140–150 spoken words
per minute, with short pauses for clicks. Record the app first, then read the
voiceover over the edited clips. Live collaboration gets the longest single shot.

## 0:00–0:10 — Why this exists

**Show:** Home, then briefly highlight the mission and project recommendations.

**Say:** “Joining a project can feel like a six-week commitment before you know
the team. Bridge Builder helps students start small, contribute across disciplines,
and discover who they work well with.”

**Caption:** Small contributions. Real connections. Proven growth.

## 0:10–0:22 — Accounts and profiles

**Show:** Two seconds of the sign-in/create-account screen; cut to a prepared
profile showing username, major, skills, goals, and contribution evidence.
Show the profile Save button without spending time typing. Crop private email.

**Say:** “Students sign in through Supabase Auth and build profiles around their
skills and goals. Registered users appear alongside clearly labeled sample
students. Public profile details help people discover one another.”

**Caption:** Supabase Auth · editable profiles · public directory

## 0:22–0:34 — Discover a useful first step

**Show:** Projects search, Role needed filter, a project card with leader and
team. Cut to Missions; select a time limit and show the reasons for a match.

**Say:** “Browse projects by skills, roles, and who posted them. Missions turn
larger projects into approachable tasks, with time estimates, difficulty,
deliverables, and clear reasons a task fits your profile.”

**Caption:** Explainable recommendations · sample project workspace

## 0:34–0:49 — Claim a mission

**Show:** In the account menu choose sample Riley Nguyen. Open “Draft an interview
guide for riders who use mobility aids.” Show the requirements and review criteria.
Click Claim mission → Yes, claim it. Hold on In Progress and Riley's ownership.

**Say:** “For this sample project, Riley chooses a research mission for an
accessible bus-stop tool. Claiming it adds Riley to the project in the right role
and gives the task an owner. Beginners can contribute through research, design,
testing, or communication.”

**Caption:** Sample workflow: claim → join project → own a task

## 0:49–1:09 — Submit, review, and prove the contribution

**Show:** Submit work with a prepared sample artifact. Cut to the sample reviewer
Grace Liu in Reviews. Open the submission, select Start review, complete the
criteria and feedback, and approve. Switch back to Riley's Profile and hold on the
new evidence card. Record these actions separately and edit out typing and menus.

**Say:** “Riley submits an artifact for review. Switching to the sample project
leader shows the reviewer flow: assess the criteria, explain what worked, and
approve or request changes. The resulting evidence card links the contribution
to the work and its feedback, so a profile shows more than a list of claimed skills.”

**Caption:** Sample reviewer flow · feedback-backed evidence

## 1:09–1:27 — Keep a cross-disciplinary team organized

**Show:** The project Team tab, then Tasks in Board, Table, and Calendar views.
Briefly open Filter and Sort. In Files, open a text file's History, Compare, and
Restore controls. Show a project channel and a task's owner/deadline.

**Say:** “Projects bring together a leader and collaborators with different roles.
Teams track owners and deadlines through board, table, and calendar views, and
coordinate in project channels. File history, text comparisons, and restore make
changes visible across documents and other kinds of work.”

**Caption:** Sample workspace · roles · tasks · channels · version history

## 1:27–1:43 — Coaching that leaves the work to the student

**Show:** Coach for Riley's project role. Send “Just do it for me.” Show the
boundary, then send a prepared attempt and show the next hint. Briefly reveal
the shared team-note area. Keep “Demo replies, no AI model” visible.

**Say:** “Role-based coaches are designed to help students think, rather than do
the assignment for them. The prototype uses scripted replies: it asks for an
attempt, offers a next step, and shares team context without exposing private
coach conversations.”

**Caption:** Coaching prototype · scripted responses, no live model

## 1:43–1:58 — Connect through real messages

**Show:** Return to Use my account. Team Up → Find a demo partner → Chat beside a
registered account. Send a short, agreed test message. Show the partner's reply
arriving automatically. Use two separately signed-in accounts for this shot.

**Say:** “Team Up connects registered students through direct messages saved in
Supabase. Replies and chat lists update automatically. Sample conversations are
separate, so a fictional preview is never mistaken for a message delivered to
another person.”

**Caption:** Live Supabase messages · automatic updates

## 1:58–2:10 — Practice before meeting

**Show:** Team Up → Practice problems. Search one topic, choose an Easy question,
and expand the prompt, demo example, and test cases. Show the FastPrep source link.

**Say:** “Before a session, students can warm up with the three-question Easy
practice bank. Each entry includes a summarized prompt, original demo examples,
cases to test, and a link to the full FastPrep question.”

**Caption:** Three Easy questions · source-linked practice

## 2:10–2:32 — Try working together

**Show:** In a real registered conversation, both users click Start demo. First
hold on the waiting lobby and two avatars. Cut to both active: the problem and
shared document appear. One user types an approach; the other adds an edge case.
Change the selected problem and show the other side update. If time allows, close
one lobby, then rejoin and show the saved draft returning.

**Say:** “The main idea is to try working together before committing to a team.
Both students enter a demo room. Supabase checks their presence before opening
the problem and shared document. One can outline an approach while the other
challenges assumptions or adds tests. Their edits and selected problem sync across
devices, and saved work is available when they reconnect.”

**Caption:** Live mode: two confirmed accounts · shared work saved to Supabase

**If only one account is available:** Record Test the shared problem editor
instead. Keep the “Local test” label visible. Replace the final two spoken
sentences with: “This local preview shows the shared editing interaction. The
registered-account flow uses Supabase presence and saved document edits.” Never
present the sample preview as footage of two real accounts.

## 2:32–2:48 — Supporting features and honest boundaries

**Show:** Team Up's progress chart, including its sample-data badge. Briefly show
its empty-state face in a separate prepared preview if available. Quick cuts:
Explore categories, global search, notifications, and light/dark appearance.

**Say:** “The interface also includes practice categories, search, notifications,
and light and dark themes. The collaboration chart currently uses sample data.
A session is framed as a thirty-minute trial; an enforced timer, peer-feedback
workflow, and automatic team conversion are next steps.”

**Caption:** Progress chart and requests: sample previews · trial completion planned

## 2:48–3:00 — Technical challenge and closing

**Show:** Two editors with matching text, then Home or the team wordmark. End with
a readable project link and team name, not the Supabase dashboard or environment file.

**Say:** “Our technical challenge was separating real accounts from demo personas
and merging shared edits without losing work. Supabase policies protect each
room. Bridge Builder makes the first contribution smaller, and choosing a team
more informed.”

**End card:** Bridge Builder · team sAI

## Recording notes

- Use a steady 16:9 crop and readable text. Give each important result a two-second hold.
- Keep typing, loading, sign-in, and persona changes out of the final cut where possible.
- Label the local mission/review/file/channel footage “Sample workspace.”
- Keep the coach's scripted-replies label and the chart's sample-data label visible.
- Do not show passwords, tokens, environment files, private emails, or unrelated chats.
- The shared editor edits plain text; it does not execute submitted code or embed Google Docs.
- Do not describe the current skill ranking as trained AI or the coach as a live model.
- Do not claim completed-demo statistics, peer feedback, or team conversion are implemented.
- Show the live two-account editor only after both people actually join. Otherwise use the labeled preview variant above.
- The repository's submission notes specify approximately three minutes, MP4, and 500 MB maximum. Confirm the final submission form before upload.
